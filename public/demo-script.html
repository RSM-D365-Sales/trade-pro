Trade Promotion Management — presenter demo script

Four acts, 27 minutes (Act 2b is optional). Tenant: Bluestem Fresh Produce, a ~$212M Michigan grower-packer-shipper and fresh-cut processor running trade promotions for its retail, club and foodservice accounts out of Excel.

Lead with the money, not the feature list. Deduction recovery is a hard-dollar, six-week sale. Full-suite TPM is a twelve-month sale. Open on Act 1 every time — planning and analytics are what expand the footprint once you are in.

Act 1

Deduction recovery — the hook

~9 min

The goal of this act is one number: $2.5M they took that nobody can justify. Everything else in the product is downstream of the room believing that number applies to them.

  1. Open Deductions. Do not narrate the nav.

    This is every chargeback your retailers and distributors took last year. Fourteen point two million dollars, three hundred documents. Right now, who researches these?

    Wait for the answer. It is almost always "Aisha in AR, part-time, and only the big ones."

  2. Point at the four buckets across the top.

    Every one of these was scored against your promotion calendar the moment it landed. A hundred and fifteen matched cleanly — nobody needs to touch those. These thirty the system thinks you should not be paying at all.

  3. Click the Likely invalid tile, then open the largest row.

    Here is why it flagged it. Customer matches, dates match, but the retailer coded it as a quality rejection — and this is a promotion. Either they miscoded real trade money, or they are charging you for soft fruit under a promotional line. Either way you do not pay it today.

  4. Scroll to the signal breakdown bars inside the drawer.

    This is the part that matters. It is not a black box giving you a number — it shows you which evidence agrees and which does not, so your analyst decides in ten seconds instead of opening four systems.

  5. Click Open dispute. Show the correspondence thread on an already-resolved one.

    Dispute, backup, correspondence, recovery — all on the document. When the buyer asks in March what happened in October, it is right here.

  6. Return to the tiles. Land the close.

    Two and a half million with nothing behind it. At the win rate this team already gets on the disputes it does open, roughly half of that comes back — and it is sitting there purely because nobody has time to research it.
Act 2

Planning — the expansion

~7 min

You are no longer selling recovery; you are selling not going back to Excel. The only thing that matters in this act is that the grid feels fast.

  1. Open Promotions → click any row.

    Six dates, and they are all different — when they can buy on deal, when we ship, when it is actually on shelf. Most spreadsheets track one. That is why deductions never reconcile.

  2. Click into a Baseline cell. Type a number. Press Enter. Watch the P&L panel move.

    Every number on the right recalculated as I typed — and it is the same calculation that runs overnight against your actuals, so what a planner sees at 9am is what finance posts at midnight.

  3. Select a range, press Ctrl+D. Then Ctrl+Z.

    Fill down. Undo. Copy and paste straight out of the deal sheet the buyer emailed you. If this grid loses to Excel, nothing else in the product matters.

  4. Point at the fund panel, then the conflicts panel.

    Live fund balance as you plan — you find out you are over-committed now, not at approval. And this flags that another event on the same customer is running the same item the same week. Two deals on one pack size double-funds the retailer and makes the lift impossible to attribute.

  5. Go to Trade calendar. Drag a bar two weeks right. Drag its trailing edge.

    Move it, extend it — snaps to retail weeks. Anything outlined in red is an item collision. And because this is produce, watch the fall apple programs stack up against each other in October.
Act 3

Honest analytics — the trust close

~6 min

This act is a differentiator, not a feature. Nearly every tool in the category reports gross lift because it flatters the buyer. Saying so out loud is the whole move.

  1. Open Trade analytics. Start at the waterfall.

    Gross to net. This is the view Marcus asks for and the one that takes three days in Excel. Everyday allowances, bill-backs, fixed funds, unrecovered deductions — then cost of goods, then what is actually left.

  2. Move to Quality of lift. Slow down here.

    Your promotions reported a certain lift. But some of it was stolen from your own sibling packs — the 3-pound pouch cannibalising the tray — and some of it was pantry loading, volume the following two weeks gave straight back. Only part of the headline was real.

    Pause. Let them react.

    Every tool in this category can show you the top number. We show you this one. You are going to make different decisions because of it.

  3. Scroll to Promotion effectiveness. Point at the struck-through ROI column.

    Struck through is what a conventional TPM tool would have reported. The number next to it is what actually happened. Several of these look positive on reported lift, which is exactly why they get repeated every year.

  4. Optional, if they are technical: open Settings and drag the amount tolerance.

    And none of this is pre-baked. That just re-scored all three hundred deductions live. Every customer tunes these against their own retailers.
Act 2b

Forecasting — for the demand planner in the room

~5 min

Skip this act if the room is all sales and finance. Run it the moment a demand planner, production planner or supply chain lead is on the call — it is the screen they will judge the whole product on.

  1. Open Forecast. Point at a single number, then at the three rows underneath it.

    Nobody typed that number. It is stores times rate of sale times seasonality times the weeks in the period. So when it is wrong, you are not arguing about a number — you are looking at which of the three assumptions broke.

  2. Point at the week count under two adjacent column headers — one 4w, one 5w.

    These are fiscal periods, not months. Four weeks, then five. A five-week period sells about twenty-five percent more at exactly the same rate of sale. If you forecast in calendar months, every period looks like a miss and nobody can tell a real problem from the calendar.

    This is the line that lands with a planner. Do not rush it.

  3. Change the level dropdown from Customer → Product group to Brand → Customer.

    Same drivers, different question. The demand planner works customer by customer; the label manager wants Orchard, Fields and Fresh Cuts the other way round. Neither of them maintains a separate spreadsheet, and the total does not move.

  4. Edit a Stores or Base velocity cell. Watch the roll-up and the total move.

    Edit at any level and it rolls straight up. And the cell stays marked as yours — the overnight recompute will not quietly overwrite a planner's judgement.

  5. Click View recommendations, then Apply on the first one.

    Twenty-one lines where the assumption has drifted from what actually shipped, of a hundred and two. Not three hundred — if everything is flagged, nobody opens it. It tells you the line, the driver, the suggested value and what it does to the number. It never changes anything on its own.

Discovery questions — ask before the demo if you can

QuestionWhy it matters
Roughly what do you spend on trade as a percent of gross?Sizes the whole opportunity. 8–15% is normal for produce once everyday allowances are counted; if they don't know, that is itself the pitch.
Who researches deductions today, and how many can they get through?Almost always one part-time person in AR doing the largest 10%. The other 90% is your number.
What happens to a deduction nobody has time to look at?"We write it off." That is the recoverable pool, stated by them.
How do you know whether a promotion worked?If the answer is "shipments went up," Act 3 will land hard.
Who builds the volume forecast, and in what?If it is a spreadsheet built off last year plus a percentage, run Act 2b.
Do you forecast in fiscal periods or calendar months?Months in a 4-4-5 business is a standing accuracy problem they may not have named.
Which ERP, and how far along are you?Bluestem runs D365 F&SC, so the connector story is native. Other ERPs land through the same CSV-first path.
How many people would touch this?Pricing is platform + trade spend under management, not pure per-seat.

Objections

They sayYou say
"Our ERP already does this." Ask them to show you fund balance by customer and label for next quarter. Trade promotion is not native to any mid-market ERP — that is why the plan lives in Excel.
"We can't afford a TPM system." Go back to the $2.5M. The system is not the cost line, the unrecovered deductions are. Start on deduction management only and expand later.
"Implementation will take a year." CSV in on day one. ERP connection when they are ready, not before. Half of mid-market prospects stay on CSV permanently and are perfectly happy.
"Our data is a mess." That is the assumption the matching engine is built on. Unmapped codes, missing references, partial claims and wrong labels are all first-class cases — this demo is full of them on purpose.
"How is this different from the big TPM suites?" Those start around six figures a year with long implementations and treat deductions as a module. For mid-market produce, deductions are the pain.

Do not do these

  • Do not open on the dashboard. It is the weakest first screen — no tension in it.
  • Do not tour the nav. Every minute in a screen that isn't Act 1 is a minute not spent on the recovery number.
  • Do not defend the demo data if they say their retailers behave differently. Say "show me a remittance file" — that is a discovery win, not an objection.
  • Do not promise the ERP connector on a date. D365 F&SC is first; everything else follows the pipeline.
Reset the demo any time from the Reset demo button on the Deductions or Settings page — it restores the seeded dataset exactly, so you can run the same script back to back.
