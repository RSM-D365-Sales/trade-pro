The story in thirty seconds

Bluestem sells $212M a year of packed and fresh-cut produce and spends about 12% of it on trade. Retailers settle that spend by short-paying invoices: $14.4M in deductions this year, and nobody has time to research them. The engine scores every chargeback against the promotion calendar on customer, date window, amount, reason code and product scope. 118 match themselves; $2.30M has no promotion behind it or is flagged as something other than trade spend, and at a 54% win rate about $1.56M is still recoverable. The same app is where Nora plans the events in a live-P&L grid, sees overlapping deals on the calendar, forecasts from drivers, and watches every fund balance fold out of its ledger.

Questions you will get

How does the matching actually work?

A weighted score over five signals: customer (22%), date window (26%), amount (22%), reason code (15%) and product scope (15%), plus a proportional lift when the retailer quotes the promotion code. Weighted rather than a decision tree so an analyst can see which signal is weak. It scores against claimable spend, because off-invoice money already left at the invoice and can never be charged back again.

Why does product scope matter so much?

With a dozen live promotions per account, some event's spend lands within tolerance of almost any deduction amount. Customer, date and amount alone will confidently pick the wrong event; label agreement is what separates the real one from the lookalike.

What makes something "likely invalid"?

Only warnings that assert the money isn't owed: a quality-rejection code against a promotion, a claim on an off-invoice-only event, or a claim above what the event can owe. An unmapped reason code is a hygiene warning and never moves a deduction into the recovery bucket.

Is the ROI real?

It's the true figure: cannibalization of sibling packs and the post-promotion dip are netted out before ranking. Analytics shows the reported ROI struck through next to it. The baseline is a 52-week moving average with promoted weeks excluded.

Where do the fund balances come from?

They are never stored. Every balance is computed from the transaction ledger (accruals, commitments on approval, reversals, settled actuals, adjustments) each time it is read, so the number and its postings can't disagree.

How does it connect to D365?

Customers, items, invoices and deductions come from F&SC, and settlements go back. The prototype runs on its own seeded data set; the connector layer is specified in the build plan and not built yet. Say so if asked.

Can we plan in fiscal periods?

Yes, that's the default. The forecast and plan run in 4-4-5 periods because a five-week period sells about 25% more than a four-week one at the same velocity; the week count sits under every column header.

If something goes wrong

  • Figures differ from this sheet. The build isn't anchored to 2026-10-07 (the Dashboard subtitle shows the as-of date). Rebuild with the three lines above; the seed is fixed, so the same numbers come back.
  • Someone accepted, disputed, dragged or applied something. Press Reset demo on Deductions or Settings, or refresh the page. Edits live in memory only.
  • The app opens in dark mode. Click the sun icon in the ribbon; the choice is remembered.
  • A port is busy. Another kit's server may hold it; change --port and open that port instead.
  • Nothing loads at all. Fall back to the looping reel while you restart the preview.
